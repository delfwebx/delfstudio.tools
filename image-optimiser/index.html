    Why your images stay private

    Most online image compressors upload your photos to their server first. This one does the work on your own computer or phone, inside the browser. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is stored.

    The lighter copies also leave out the hidden details cameras attach to photos, such as the place a picture was taken and the device that took it. That makes them safer to post and share.

    Which settings to pick

    • For email: Balanced with the Email size. Most photos land well under 500 KB.
    • For a website: Balanced with the Web size, saved as WebP if your site supports it.
    • For an upload form with a limit: Custom size, set just below the limit.
    • For printing: Light and Keep original, so no detail is lost to resizing.

    Questions people ask

    How much smaller will my photos get?

    A typical camera or phone photo of 5 to 12 MB comes down to a few hundred KB at the Web or Email size, which is 90 percent or more. Keeping the original picture size saves less, usually a half to three quarters.

    Can I download one image without waiting for the rest?

    Yes. Each image gets its own Download button the moment it is ready. When two or more are finished you can also take them together as one ZIP file.

    Can I rename the files?

    Yes. Click any file name in the list to change it, or use Name all files to give a whole batch the same name with numbers.

    Which image types can I use?

    JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF and most other common formats. HEIC photos from iPhones open in Safari but not in most other browsers. RAW files from DSLR cameras need exporting to JPEG first.

    What is the difference between JPEG, WebP and PNG?

    JPEG opens everywhere and is the safe choice for photos and email. WebP makes smaller files and suits websites, but some older email programs cannot show it. PNG keeps see-through backgrounds and sharp graphics, but photos saved as PNG stay large.

    Is there a limit on how many images I can do?

    No limit from us. Images are handled one after another, so a big batch simply takes a little longer. Very large photos may be too much for older phones at full size, in which case choose a smaller picture size.

    Does it cost anything?

    No. It is free to use, with no sign-up and no watermark on your images.
